# Reviewer Lens — Community Data Fellowship 2027 (Lanterne Foundation)

**Doc type / reviewer / decision:** Grant/fellowship call · two reviewers per application, at least one a former fellow, ~20 min each, dozens-to-hundreds of applications for 8–10 awards · fund
**Filter or confirmation:** Filter — reviewers hunt for the pattern they have "seen too many" of (a platform with a community attached); the shortlist call, with a community member present, is the confirmation step.

**Veto criterion:** "This is a platform the applicant wants to build with a community attached as a user base; swap the community and it still makes sense, so it is a product, and we fund people, not products." Community ownership recurs in about sixteen sentences across all five sections, carries 50% of the score, and is verified twice (work-sample role split, community member on the call) — and "a civic-tech idea I've wanted to build for a while" is the excluded pattern almost verbatim.

**Reviewer's mental model:**
I've been a fellow, and this is my fortieth narrative. Most open with the idea; I skip to find the community's name, how long, and whether *they* asked — if they appear only as users, point 1 is low before I reach the handover. Then I look for a person's name next to "who keeps the spreadsheet." What the narrative can't tell me is whether this applicant can let a community own the question; the call is where I find out.

## Implicit requirements
- The community originated the question; remove them and the project collapses — "If your project would still make sense with a different community swapped in, it is probably a product." — **Stated**
- The relationship pre-dates the application and will be audited live — "we ask that a community member join" — **Stated**
- Your narrative alone is weak evidence that they asked — "what we need is evidence that they asked for this, or shaped it with you" — **Stated**
- Handover means named people and their existing tools — "Who can run the survey again next year without you?" — **Stated**
- A tool is a means and cannot be how people participate — "they can be a means, not the end" — **Stated**
- The budget proves "with": community members are paid — "the majority to go to your time and to community participants' time" — **Strong**
- You are the investment; the consultant's posture is what changes — "how the fellowship will change their practice" — **Strong**

## Rubric
| Criterion | Weight | Conf. | Failing looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Existing relationship: named, dated, in-capacity, they asked | 30% | Stated | "I plan to partner with local groups" |
| Community's question, in their words, tied to their decision | 20% | Stated | Your problem statement that your tool solves |
| Handover: named custodians, existing tools, repeatable without you | 30% | Stated | "We will publish a report" |
| What you will learn: a change to your practice | 20% | Stated | Omitted, or "I'll gain experience" |
| Budget fit: majority to time incl. paid community time; tools ≤15% | Gate | Stated | Developer time and hosting dominate |
| Not a product: passes the swap test; no new software to participate | Gate | Stated | Opens with the platform and a roadmap |
| Work sample made *with* a community; honest role split | Gate | Stated | "I built it, they used it" |

**Not graded (or tiebreaker only):** the idea's novelty, technical design, scale, outcome metrics (never asked for — don't import a grant "impact" section), your credentials, a prototype.
**Differentiators:** the question as a verbatim quote from a named person; a handover table with a dry-run month where they run it without you; a learning statement naming what a consultant must unlearn.

## What to show
| Criterion | Surface | Proof moment | Anti-pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relationship | Narrative, first paragraph | "Since [date] I have [capacity] with [community]; in [month], [name, role] asked whether [question]." | "I plan to partner with…" |
| Community's question | Narrative, pull-quote | Their words, attributed, plus the decision the answer informs | Your problem statement |
| Handover | Narrative, a table | Artifact → custodian (name) → their existing tool → next-year owner → dry-run month | "Publish a report" |
| Learning | Narrative, short section | "I have only delivered to clients; I want to learn to work where I don't set the question." | "I'll learn new tools" |
| Budget fit | One-page budget | Three visible shares: your time; community time at a stated rate; tools ≤15% | A development budget |
| Not a product | One sentence in the question section | "If [community] disappeared, this project would not exist"; tool named once, as a means | Architecture, roadmap, "scalable" |
| Work sample + letters | Caption; letters; call | "My role: X. Their role: they chose the questions and decided." Letters that say "we asked" | "Their role was to use it" |

**Required-to-exist events:**
- "how long you have worked with them and in what capacity" — the relationship exists before you write — now
- "evidence that they asked for this, or shaped it with you" — a conversation where they set the question — before 20 Dec
- "in their words" — you captured their wording — same conversation
- "Who keeps the spreadsheet? … next year without you?" — two named people have agreed — before drafting handover
- "something you have made with a community before" — a with-community artifact exists — identify now; if none, ask (Decisions §6)
- "a community member join" — someone willing to sit the call — confirm before submitting

**Simulated verdict (if the plan is followed):** "Opened with the group and their question in their own words, named who keeps the spreadsheet, and was honest that this is their first project where the community owns the result — shortlist, and ask [name] to join the call."
**Simulated verdict (if the current approach is followed):** "A civic-tech platform the applicant has wanted to build, with a community still to be attached. It would make sense anywhere. Not a fit."

## Explicit requirements
- [MUST] Narrative ≤1,500 words covering points 1–5, "in any order you like; headings help us".
- [MUST] Point 1: a specific community, existing relationship, how long, in what capacity, evidence they asked or shaped it.
- [MUST] Point 2: a question the community wants answered — [SHOULD] "in their words".
- [MUST] Point 3: how the community holds data and results after the fellowship — "'We will publish a report' is not a plan."
- [MUST] Point 4: what you will learn; "Fellows are selected partly on how the fellowship will change their practice."
- [MUST] Point 5 and a one-page budget: ≤$40,000 over 12 months; majority to your time and community participants' time; tools "rarely… more than 15%". Checked for fit, not scored.
- [MUST] One work sample made with a community (map, report, survey instrument, workshop plan) plus two sentences: your role, the community's role.
- [MAY] Up to two letters from community members.
- [MUST NOT] Applicant as primary beneficiary of the data; research *on* rather than *with*; platform/app/dashboard as the deliverable; participation that requires new software.
- [MUST] Deadline 15 January; receipt confirmed within two days; decision by 1 March.
- [MAY] Fit questions to fellowship@lanterne.org before 20 December; anonymised answers published weekly on the call page.
- [SHOULD] Be ready for a 30-minute shortlist conversation with two reviewers, with a community member joining "where possible".

## Traps
- **The swap test is the reviewer's embedded diagnostic, and your idea as described fails it by default.** "A civic-tech idea I've wanted to build" is applicant-originated, community-agnostic and tool-shaped. Run the test before writing a word: if the idea only makes sense because of one community's question, it is a means; if it makes sense anywhere, this is the wrong funder for it.
- **Over-delivery trap:** every sentence on the tool (features, architecture, prototype, roadmap, open-sourcing "so others can use it") confirms "product". Reuse elsewhere is precisely the failing property, not a strength.
- **"Applicant is the primary beneficiary":** a consultancy reads as portfolio, IP or lead-generation by default. Say outright that data, results and any tool belong to the community and the consultancy takes nothing forward.
- **"Require the community to adopt new software to participate":** if the tool is how people take part, the project is excluded. Participation runs on what they already use (paper, WhatsApp, their spreadsheet); the tool, if any, sits behind that.
- **Budget fit is unscored but gating:** a build-heavy budget (>15% tools, developer time dominant, no community-time line) fails "fit" silently.
- **The work-sample role split exposes "with" vs "for":** a client dashboard where the community's role was "client" tells a former fellow you have not done this before. Honesty here is read; inflation is caught on the call.
- **The shortlist call is a live audit of point 1.** A relationship described as deeper or longer than it is unravels in 30 minutes; a short but real one, honestly dated, does not.
- **"Any order you like" is read as your priority order.** Opening with the idea opens with the product.
- **Tone trap:** the call is plain, concrete and example-led, and uses "user base" pejoratively. Grant-speak ("empower", "stakeholders", "scalable", "impact", "leverage") and civic-tech vocabulary ("users", "platform", "MVP") mismatch the register and pattern-match the scar.
- **Archetype import:** typical grant panels veto on vague impact; this call never asks for outcomes or metrics. An "expected impact" section is ungraded words taken out of the 1,500.
- **Timing:** the "they asked for this" conversation and any fit question must happen before 20 December. The published Q&A page is a free expansion of the rubric; read it before drafting.
- **Under-specified term — "community" is never defined.** The examples are membership or place groups (neighbourhood association, tenants' union) or a staff group (library staff). A city department or NGO client is an institution; if your relationship is institutional, name the people-community served by it and show the relationship with *them*.

## Decisions to make / questions to ask
Asking is invited ("Questions about fit are welcome… We answer every one"), so each item is marked Ask or Assume. Ask before 20 December; answers are anonymised and published.

1. Does the civic-tech idea survive the swap test? → **Decide first, honestly.** If it only makes sense because of a specific community's question, demote it to one sentence as a means. If it makes sense anywhere, do not lead with it: apply with a community-originated question, or accept this is the wrong funder for the product. — Rationale: "not a fit" is stated twice.
2. Which community? → **Decide.** The group you have the longest genuine relationship with — inside the consultancy's work or outside it — that is a community (residents, members, staff) rather than a client institution. — Rationale: 30% is "depth of the existing relationship"; depth cannot be manufactured by January.
3. If the relationship is short (weeks or months) → **Ask:** "Does a relationship of [N] months, where the group has asked for help with [question], count as an existing relationship for this call?" — Rationale: cheaper to learn fit now than to lose 30%; the Foundation publishes answers precisely so applicants ask.
4. Where the tool goes → **Assume:** one sentence, in the question or handover section, as a means, running on software the community already uses. — Rationale: "means, not the end"; the new-software exclusion.
5. Letters → **Assume: get one or two,** in the community's own words, stating what they asked you for. — Rationale: "not required," but "what we need is evidence that they asked for this"; the letter is the cheapest strong evidence.
6. Work sample → **Decide, then Ask if needed.** Pick the prior artifact where the community's role was largest. If all prior work is client-for, ask: "Would a [survey/report] made for [organisation], where [its members] designed the questions, count as made with a community?" — Rationale: the two-sentence role split is the test.
7. Who keeps the spreadsheet; who runs it next year → **Decide with the community, now.** Name a person and their existing tool. — Rationale: point 3's two questions *are* the handover rubric.
8. Budget split → **Assume** roughly 55–60% your time, 20–25% community participants' time at a stated hourly rate, ≤10% tools, remainder venue, travel, printing, translation. — Rationale: "majority to your time and community participants' time"; "rarely… more than 15% on tools".
9. Section order → **Assume** relationship → question → handover → learning → budget note. — Rationale: the reviewer hunts in weight order; veto proof goes first.
10. The consultancy's stake → **Assume and state:** no IP, no reuse, no case study without permission; the fellowship pays you as an individual. — Rationale: "applicant is the primary beneficiary" exclusion; "we fund people".
11. Who joins the shortlist call → **Decide now:** the person who asked the question. — Rationale: "we ask that a community member join".

## Recommended shape
Narrative of up to 1,500 words, with headings that mirror the five points so each scored line is easy to find.

| Section (in reading order) | Share | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| 1. [Community name] and how we work together | ~450 words (30%) | Name, start date, capacity; the moment they asked; pointer to the work sample and letters |
| 2. Their question | ~250 words (20%) | Verbatim quote, attributed; the decision it informs; why their existing data can't answer it; the swap-test sentence; the tool in one line as a means |
| 3. Who holds the data and results afterwards | ~450 words (30%) | Handover table: artifact → custodian (name) → existing tool → who runs it next year → month-9 dry run without you; what happens if you disappear |
| 4. What I will learn | ~250 words (20%) | The specific shift from delivering to clients to working where the community sets the question; what you expect to find hard |
| 5. Budget (summary; full page attached) | ~100 words | Shares to your time, community participants' time at a rate, tools ≤15% |

Budget page: three visible blocks mirroring the summary. Work-sample caption: two sentences, honest role split. Letters: ask each writer to say what they asked you for and why, in their words, not to praise you.

## Pre-submission check
- [ ] Narrative ≤1,500 words, five headings mapping to points 1–5
- [ ] First paragraph names the community, the start date, your capacity, and who asked
- [ ] The community's question appears as a quote attributed to a person or role
- [ ] Swap-test sentence present; tool mentioned once, as a means, on software they already use
- [ ] Handover section has named custodians, existing tools, a next-year owner, and a dry-run month
- [ ] Learning section names a change to your consulting practice, not a skill acquisition
- [ ] Budget is one page; majority to time; community participants' time at a stated rate; tools ≤15%
- [ ] Work sample is with-community; the two role sentences are honest
- [ ] One or two letters say "we asked"
- [ ] A community member has agreed to join the shortlist call
- [ ] No "users", "stakeholders", "leverage", "scalable", "impact", roadmap, or reuse-elsewhere claims
- [ ] Fit questions sent before 20 Dec; the published Q&A read

**Sequencing:**
- Now, before 20 Dec: run the swap test; choose the community; read the published Q&A; send any fit questions; meet the community with nothing to pitch and ask what they want to know — write down their words; ask for letters.
- First third: agree custodians and the next-year owner with the community; draft the budget with their time in it.
- Halfway: write the narrative in the order above; cut the tool to one sentence.
- Last third: collect letters; confirm the call participant; choose and caption the work sample.
- Before 15 Jan: word count; headings; first-paragraph check against the veto.
