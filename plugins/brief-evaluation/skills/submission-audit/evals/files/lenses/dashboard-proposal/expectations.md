# Evaluator Lens — "Need a Shopify analytics dashboard built — FAST"

## 1. Description

A non-technical owner of a mid-size Shopify store selling home fitness gear wants a freelancer to build a self-updating analytics dashboard (§intro — "about 3,000 orders/month, two staff"; "It should update itself").
It replaces four hours every Monday of pulling Shopify, Meta, Google and Klaviyo numbers into a Google Sheet (§intro — "Every Monday I spend 4 hours").
The dashboard must show revenue/orders/AOV by day/week/month, ad spend and ROAS per channel, Klaviyo email revenue, top 10 products and refund rate (§"I want a simple dashboard").
The submission is a written proposal that must start with the name of a fitness product for someone who hates the gym, then say what you'd build, roughly how long, and what you'd need from the client (§"To make sure you read this…"; §"Tell me what you'd build, roughly how long, and what you'd need from me").
It must show at least one prior Shopify integration (§Must have — "(show me)"), in plain language with no framework list (§"I'm not technical. Please don't send me a list of frameworks.").
Budget is $1,500 fixed, and the work should land before the Black Friday push, about 5 weeks out (§header — "Budget: $1,500 fixed"; §Timeline).
The client picks one freelancer by end of this week from 20 to 50 proposals (§header — "Proposals: 20 to 50"; §close — "I'll pick someone by end of this week").
Underneath, the decision is about trust: the last freelancer's dashboard broke and he stopped answering (§"he stopped answering messages").

## 2. Context understanding

- **Type / archetype:** client-brief, a freelance marketplace job post with a fixed budget — §header — "Budget: $1,500 fixed | Experience level: Intermediate"
- **Evaluator:** the store owner, writing in the first person, non-technical (§"I'm not technical"); compares 20–50 proposals (§header), about 30 seconds each on first pass (inferred from volume and a one-person shop)
- **Decision:** hire one freelancer (§close — "I'll pick someone by end of this week") · **Filter or confirmation:** hard filter. Two gates (the product opener and "(show me)") decide whether the proposal is read at all; the veto decides whether it wins.
- **Constraints:** $1,500 fixed (§header); "Intermediate" level (§header); about 5 weeks to the Black Friday push (§Timeline), which is ~Nov 9–12 from a post dated ~Oct 5 ("Posted: 3 days ago", inferred date), so ~4 weeks from a hire this week; stack open (§"I don't care what it's built with"); format is a proposal in plain language that answers three questions in order (§"Tell me what you'd build…").
- **Stakes and tone:** casual, direct, slightly worn out ("Hi there", "drowning in spreadsheets", "Thanks!"). The client was burned before and says so; plain, direct prose with a little personality matches, formal cover-letter register does not.
- **User's context:** bidding as a solo freelancer with 6 years of Shopify/analytics work; deadline is the post closing at the end of this week. No prior attempt or message to this client yet.

## 3. Evaluator intent

**In their words (first person):** I run a home-fitness store with two staff and read these between orders. No fitness product in line one, or "Shopify experience" with nothing to look at, and I close it. I already paid for a dashboard that fell over when Shopify changed something and watched the guy stop replying, so I'm reading for whether you'll still answer me in February, in words I can repeat. Whoever warns me first that Meta and Google both claim the same sale gets my reply.

**What they actually want:** not a dashboard but the end of the Monday chore *and* someone who will not disappear. The sentence the client flags as "what I actually need" names durability and reachability ("something that keeps working, and someone who will tell me if it's going to break"); the post's only story is losing both (Looker Studio "broke every time Shopify changed something", "he stopped answering messages"); and "It should update itself" / "I don't want to click anything on Monday morning except 'open'" repeat the first half. Four sentences across the feature list and the need statement point at one theme.

**Weightiest themes:**
- Durability + reachability after launch — ×4 across the feature list and the need statement — §"It should update itself", §"keeps working, and someone who will tell me if it's going to break"
- Plain-language communication for a non-technical reader — ×3 — §"I'm not technical. Please don't send me a list of frameworks.", §"Honestly I don't care what it's built with", §"Tell me what you'd build…"
- Proof, not claims — ×2 — §Must have — "(show me)"; §"To make sure you read this…"
- Their problem, their numbers — ×2 — §"Every Monday I spend 4 hours…", §"so I can see what's actually making money"
- Speed / timing — ×3 — title "FAST", §Timeline, §"I'll pick someone by end of this week"

**Not graded (or tiebreaker only):** the stack ("don't care"), visual polish, portfolio breadth, years of experience, real-time data, anything beyond the five numbers.

### Deal breakers

**Veto criterion:** Nothing here told me it would keep working when Shopify changes something, or that you'd still answer my messages when it doesn't. In the client's shorthand: *"Fluent, but you never said what happens after you hand it over — and the last guy didn't either."* The evidence: the sentence flagged "what I actually need" names durability and reachability (§"something that keeps working, and someone who will tell me if it's going to break"), the post's only story is losing both (§"broke every time Shopify changed something and then he stopped answering messages"), and "It should update itself" / "don't click anything … except 'open'" repeat the first — four sentences across the feature list and the need statement.

- **Attention filter placed late** (embedded filter) — §"To make sure you read this, start your proposal with the name of a fitness product…" — it sits after the requirements to catch skimmers. It is also a mini market test: "someone who hates the gym" is *their* customer (home fitness), so a gym product (a barbell) reads as not getting it; a home product (walking pad, adjustable dumbbells, resistance bands) does. Put it as word one, before any greeting.
- **"(show me)" is a proof gate** (embedded filter) — §Must have — a sentence of experience without a link, screenshot, or named store is a claim, and claims don't pass. Name one specific Shopify build.
- **Stack trap** (over-delivery / tone) — §"I don't care what it's built with" + §"Please don't send me a list of frameworks" — every tool name spends words on an announced non-criterion. Proposing Looker Studio without addressing the breakage story is the tone-deaf version. Describe by outcome; at most one tool in a parenthesis.
- **Fixed fee vs open-ended "tell me if it's going to break"** (tension / planted obstacle) — §header "$1,500 fixed" vs §"someone who will tell me if it's going to break" — the last freelancer did fixed work, then had no reason to keep answering. If the after-care isn't bounded in writing, you are signing up for the same ending.
- **Attribution ground is unstable** (planted obstacle) — §"Ad spend and ROAS per channel", §"Email revenue from Klaviyo" — Meta, Google and Klaviyo each report revenue by their own attribution window; per-channel numbers will add to more than Shopify revenue. The client will notice on Monday one. Warn now, or lose trust then.
- **Date math** (planted obstacle) — §Timeline — "before our Black Friday push" is ~Nov 9–12, not Nov 27; five weeks from an ~Oct 5 post, and ~4 weeks from a hire this week. Black Friday week is also the highest-volume, highest-stakes week — the dashboard must be *proven* before it, not finished on it.
- **Timing** (blocks-review mechanic) — §header "Posted: 3 days ago", §close "I'll pick someone by end of this week" — a proposal sent tomorrow may be too late, and the first client reply tests reachability from message one. Notifications on, answer within hours.
- **Over-delivery** — §"I want a simple dashboard" — real-time refresh, twenty metrics, forecasting, "I'll also fix your attribution model," or a discovery phase before quoting: each blows "simple," the budget, or the "answer me now" ask.
- **Graded but looks optional** — the wit of the opener, the specificity of the access list, naming running costs, the one "what breaks" sentence, who owns the accounts.
- **Tone** — §"Hi there", §"Thanks!" — invites plain, direct prose with a little personality. "Dear Hiring Manager, I am writing to express my interest" is a mismatch they notice before content.
- **Defects in the stated list** (noticing is a differentiator) — §feature list — "ROAS per channel" with no truth source; Klaviyo revenue double-counts ad-attributed revenue; refund rate undefined (by count or value, by order date or refund date); top 10 by what; refresh cadence unstated; "what's actually making money" hints at margin but no COGS is mentioned.
- **Blocks review entirely** — no product opener; no Shopify evidence; price far outside $1,500; arriving after the pick.

### Deal makers

**Differentiators:**
- Naming the attribution over-claim and a blended ROAS as the truth number — §"Ad spend and ROAS per channel", §"what's actually making money"
- A check the client runs themselves: two Mondays beside their Sheet — §"pulling numbers … into a Google Sheet"
- Landing a week early — §Timeline

**Decisions to make / questions to ask** (ordered by stakes; there is no Q&A window, so assume and state; reserve at most two questions for the "what I'd need from you" section, where asking is sanctioned):
- **After-care bound** → Decide: 30 days of fixes after go-live (covers BF and Cyber Monday) included; then an optional small monthly check (API-version watch, "you'll hear from me first") at a named price — answers the ghosting fear with a schedule, not a promise, and resolves fixed-fee vs open-ended.
- **Cadence during build** → Decide: a short Friday update message, no meetings unless they want one — reachability demonstrated weekly before launch is the only evidence they can see before February.
- **Stack** → Assume; describe by outcome, name at most one tool in a parenthesis — whichever you pick, the proposal should say three things: you own the data-pull code (no third-party connector — that is what broke last time), it lands somewhere they already open, and it pins to a current API version with a quarterly check.
- **ROAS truth source** → Assume: show each platform's own ROAS *and* a blended ROAS (Shopify revenue ÷ total ad spend) as the headline — "what's actually making money" is a blended question; say up front the channel numbers will over-claim and why.
- **Timeline shape** → Decide: Shopify numbers live in week 2; ads and Klaviyo week 3; parallel run against their Sheet on two Mondays; go-live target ~Nov 2 — lands a week before the push and gives them a check they can run themselves.
- **Scope boundary** → Decide: the five numbers from the four sources; excluded: margin/COGS, other sales channels, inventory, forecasting — note margin as a cheap later add if cost-per-item is filled in Shopify — "simple" and $1,500 are the same test.
- **Running costs and ownership** → Decide: all accounts and credentials in the client's name; name the monthly cost even if ~$0 — durability that survives you is the strongest answer to the Looker story.
- **Price** → $1,500 fixed, matched — far outside in either direction is a veto; slightly above with a reason is fine.
- **Klaviyo** → If you have it, name where; if not, say so and that it is the simplest of the four sources — an honest "no" on a nice-to-have is a trust signal; a faked "yes" is the opposite.
- **Refresh cadence** → Assume nightly — Monday-morning use; avoids rate limits during BF volume.
- **Default view** → Assume opens to this week vs last week, month to date; day/week/month toggle — "open" is the only click they want to make.
- **Historical backfill** → Decide: 13 months — lets them compare this Black Friday to last year's, which is the Monday after the push.
- **Refund rate** → Assume refunded orders ÷ orders in period, by order date, with refund value shown too — cheap to state; shows you saw the ambiguity.
- **Top 10 products** → Assume by revenue in the selected period, units alongside — one line, stated.
- **Ask (1):** a copy of the current Monday Sheet — it is both the spec and the parallel-run baseline.
- **Ask (2):** the date the Black Friday push actually starts — fixes the real deadline.
