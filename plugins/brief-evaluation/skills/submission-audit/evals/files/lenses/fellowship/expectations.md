# Evaluator Lens — Community Data Fellowship 2027

## 1. Description

The Lanterne Foundation is choosing 8–10 practitioners for awards of up to $40,000 over 12 months (§header — "Awards of up to $40,000 · 8–10 fellows"; §What we are looking for, point 5).
It funds people who help a community use its own data, where "the community owned the question, the data, and the result" (§About the fellowship).
The application is a narrative of up to 1,500 words covering the call's five numbered points, a one-page budget, one work sample with a two-sentence role split, and up to two optional community letters (§Application).
Two evaluators read each application in full and score relationship 30%, question 20%, handover 30% and learning 20%; budget is "checked for fit but not scored" (§How we review).
Shortlisted applicants get a 30-minute conversation, ideally with a community member present (§How we review — "we ask that a community member join").
Platforms, apps or dashboards as the deliverable, and research *on* a community, are excluded outright (§What we are not looking for).
The deadline is 15 January; fit questions are answered if sent before 20 December, and decisions arrive by 1 March (§Application; closing paragraph).

## 2. Context understanding

- **Type / archetype:** grant / fellowship open call; funds the person, not the project — §About the fellowship — "We fund people, not products."
- **Evaluator:** two evaluators per application, at least one a former fellow — §How we review — "Every application is read in full by two reviewers, at least one of whom has been a fellow." Inferred: dozens to hundreds of applications for 8–10 awards, ~20 minutes each.
- **Decision:** fund (shortlist, then award) · **Filter or confirmation:** filter — evaluators hunt for the pattern they have "seen too many" of (a platform with a community attached); the shortlist call with a community member is the confirmation step.
- **Constraints:** narrative ≤1,500 words; budget one page, ≤$40,000 over 12 months, majority to applicant and community participants' time, tools rarely above 15%; one work sample; deadline 15 January; fit questions before 20 December — §Application; §What we are looking for, point 5.
- **Stakes and tone:** plain, concrete, example-led; uses "user base" pejoratively and names its scar tissue openly ("We have seen too many proposals…"). Grant-speak and civic-tech vocabulary mismatch the register.
- **User's context:** runs a small data consultancy and has a civic-tech idea they have wanted to build; deadline 15 January. The idea, as described, is applicant-originated and tool-shaped — the excluded pattern almost verbatim.

## 3. Evaluator intent

**In their words (first person):** I've been a fellow, and this is my fortieth narrative. Most open with the idea; I skip to find the community's name, how long, and whether *they* asked — if they appear only as users, point 1 is low before I reach the handover. Then I look for a person's name next to "who keeps the spreadsheet." What the narrative can't tell me is whether this applicant can let a community own the question; the call is where I find out.

**What they actually want:** a practitioner whose community already owns the question, the data and the result, and who will leave them able to continue without the fellow. Community ownership recurs in about sixteen sentences across all five sections, carries 50% of the score (relationship + handover), and is verified twice (work-sample role split, community member on the call).

**Weightiest themes:**
- Community ownership / "with, not for" — ×~16 across About, Looking for, Not looking for, How we review, Application — §About the fellowship — "the community owned the question, the data, and the result"
- Existing relationship, evidenced — 30% weight plus the call and letters — §What we are looking for, point 1
- Handover without the fellow — 30% weight — §What we are looking for, point 3 — "Who can run the survey again next year without you?"
- Not a product — stated twice as "not a fit" / excluded — §About the fellowship; §What we are not looking for
- The fellow's own change — 20% — §What we are looking for, point 4

**Not graded (or tiebreaker only):** the idea's novelty, technical design, scale, outcome metrics (never asked for — do not import a grant "impact" section), credentials, a prototype.

### Deal breakers

**Veto criterion:** This is a platform the applicant wants to build with a community attached as a user base; swap the community and it still makes sense, so it is a product, and we fund people, not products. Evidence: §About the fellowship — "We have seen too many proposals that describe a platform the applicant wants to build, with a community attached as a user base. Those are not a fit here." and the swap test; §What we are not looking for — "Platforms, apps, or dashboards as the deliverable"; ownership carries 50% of the score and is verified by the work-sample role split and the community member on the call. "A civic-tech idea I've wanted to build" is the excluded pattern almost verbatim.

- **Embedded filter — the swap test, which the idea as described fails by default.** "A civic-tech idea I've wanted to build" is applicant-originated, community-agnostic and tool-shaped — §About the fellowship — "If your project would still make sense with a different community swapped in, it is probably a product." — Run the test before writing a word: if the idea only makes sense because of one community's question, it is a means; if it makes sense anywhere, this is the wrong funder for it, and not applying this cycle is an honest option.
- **Over-delivery trap:** every sentence on the tool (features, architecture, prototype, roadmap, open-sourcing "so others can use it") confirms "product"; reuse elsewhere is precisely the failing property — §What we are not looking for — "they can be a means, not the end" — Cut the tool to one sentence.
- **"Applicant is the primary beneficiary":** a consultancy reads as portfolio, IP or lead generation by default — §What we are not looking for — "Projects where the applicant is the primary beneficiary of the data" — Say outright that data, results and any tool belong to the community and the consultancy takes nothing forward.
- **New-software exclusion:** if the tool is how people take part, the project is excluded — §What we are not looking for — "Projects that require the community to adopt new software to participate" — Participation runs on what they already use (paper, WhatsApp, their spreadsheet); any tool sits behind that.
- **Budget fit is unscored but gating:** a build-heavy budget (>15% tools, developer time dominant, no community-time line) fails "fit" silently — §What we are looking for, point 5 — "we rarely fund more than 15% on tools" — Show three visible shares: your time, community time at a rate, tools.
- **The work-sample role split exposes "with" vs "for":** a client dashboard where the community's role was "client" tells a former fellow you have not done this before — §Application — "what your role was and what the community's role was" — Pick the artifact where their role was largest; be honest, inflation is caught on the call.
- **The shortlist call is a live audit of point 1:** a relationship described as deeper or longer than it is unravels in 30 minutes; a short but real one, honestly dated, does not — §How we review — "Shortlisted applicants will have a 30-minute conversation with two reviewers" — Date the relationship accurately.
- **Institutional relationship mistaken for a community (under-specified term):** "community" is never defined; the examples are membership or place groups (neighbourhood association, tenants' union) or a staff group (library staff) — §About the fellowship — A council, city department or NGO client is an institution; paid workshops or consulting for a council are not a relationship with the named community. Name the people-community served and show the relationship with *them*; if none exists yet, do not apply this cycle.
- **"Any order you like" is read as your priority order:** opening with the idea opens with the product — §Application — Lead with the relationship.
- **Tone trap:** grant-speak ("empower", "stakeholders", "scalable", "impact", "leverage") and civic-tech vocabulary ("users", "platform", "MVP") mismatch the register and pattern-match the scar — §About the fellowship — "with a community attached as a user base" — Write plainly, with concrete examples.
- **Archetype import:** typical grant panels veto on vague impact; this call never asks for outcomes or metrics — §What we are looking for (no outcomes item) — An "expected impact" section is ungraded words taken out of the 1,500.
- **Timing:** the "they asked for this" conversation and any fit question must happen before 20 December — closing paragraph — "before 20 December" — Read the published Q&A page (a free expansion of the rubric) before drafting.

### Deal makers

**Differentiators:**
- The question as a verbatim quote from a named person — §What we are looking for, point 2 — "in their words if possible"
- A handover table with a dry-run month where the community runs it without you — §What we are looking for, point 3 — "without you"
- A learning statement naming what a consultant must unlearn — §What we are looking for, point 4

**Decisions to make / questions to ask** (ordered by stakes; asking is invited — "We answer every one" — so each is marked Ask or Assume; ask before 20 December, answers are anonymised and published):
- Does the civic-tech idea survive the swap test? → **Decide first, honestly.** If it only makes sense because of a specific community's question, demote it to one sentence as a means. If it makes sense anywhere, do not lead with it: apply with a community-originated question, or accept this is the wrong funder for the product and do not apply this cycle. — "Not a fit" is stated twice.
- Which community? → **Decide.** The group you have the longest genuine relationship with — inside the consultancy's work or outside it — that is a community (residents, members, staff) rather than a client institution. — 30% is "depth of the existing relationship"; depth cannot be manufactured by January.
- If the relationship is short (weeks or months) → **Ask:** "Does a relationship of [N] months, where the group has asked for help with [question], count as an existing relationship for this call?" — Cheaper to learn fit now than to lose 30%; the Foundation publishes answers precisely so applicants ask.
- Who keeps the spreadsheet; who runs it next year → **Decide with the community, now.** Name a person and their existing tool. — Point 3's two questions *are* the handover rubric.
- Work sample → **Decide, then Ask if needed.** Pick the prior artifact where the community's role was largest. If all prior work is client-for, ask: "Would a [survey/report] made for [organisation], where [its members] designed the questions, count as made with a community?" — The two-sentence role split is the test.
- Where the tool goes → **Assume:** one sentence, in the question or handover section, as a means, running on software the community already uses. — "a means, not the end"; the new-software exclusion.
- The consultancy's stake → **Assume and state:** no IP, no reuse, no case study without permission; the fellowship pays you as an individual. — The "primary beneficiary" exclusion; "We fund people".
- Budget split → **Assume** roughly 55–60% your time, 20–25% community participants' time at a stated hourly rate, ≤10% tools, remainder venue, travel, printing, translation. — "the majority to go to your time and to community participants' time"; "rarely… more than 15% on tools".
- Letters → **Assume: get one or two,** in the community's own words, stating what they asked you for. — "not required", but "what we need is evidence that they asked for this"; a letter is the cheapest strong evidence.
- Section order → **Assume** relationship → question → handover → learning → budget note. — The evaluator hunts in weight order; veto proof goes first.
- Who joins the shortlist call → **Decide now:** the person who asked the question. — "we ask that a community member join".
