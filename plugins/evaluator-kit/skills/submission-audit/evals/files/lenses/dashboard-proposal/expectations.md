# Evaluator Lens — "Need a Shopify analytics dashboard built — FAST"

**Doc type / evaluator / decision:** Freelance client brief ($1,500 fixed) · non-technical owner-operator, 20–50 proposals, ~30 seconds each · hire one freelancer by end of this week
**Filter or confirmation:** Hard filter — two gates (product opener, "show me") decide whether you are read; the veto decides whether you win.

**Veto criterion:** *"Fluent, but you never said what happens after you hand it over — and the last guy didn't either."* The sentence flagged "what I actually need" names durability and reachability, the post's only story is losing both, and "update itself" / "don't click anything except open" repeat the first — four sentences across the feature list and the need statement.

**Evaluator's mental model:**
I run a home-fitness store with two staff and read these between orders. No fitness product in line one, or "Shopify experience" with nothing to look at, and I close it. I already paid for a dashboard that fell over when Shopify changed something and watched the guy stop replying, so I'm reading for whether you'll still answer me in February, in words I can repeat. Whoever warns me first that Meta and Google both claim the same sale gets my reply.

## Implicit requirements
- Post-launch trust: what breaks, what you do first, and a contact cadence — "something that keeps working, and someone who will tell me if it's going to break" — **Stated**
- The proposal is the communication test: outcome language, no tool names, three answers in their order — "I'm not technical. Please don't send me a list of frameworks." — **Stated**
- Prove listening: restate their problem in their numbers (4 hours, Monday, four sources) — "To make sure you read this…" — **Strong**
- Experience shown, not claimed: a named Shopify build with something to click — "done at least one Shopify integration before (show me)" — **Stated**
- Scope definition is graded: "simple" and $1,500 against four live integrations; say what's in, out, and the running cost — **Strong**
- Bound the fixed fee against "tell me if it's going to break," which has no end date — the collision that produced the last ghosting — **Strong**
- Warn about attribution before Monday one: Meta, Google and Klaviyo each claim the same orders, so "ROAS per channel" has no truth source — **Inferred**

## Rubric
| Criterion | Weight | Conf. | Failing looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opener is a home-fitness product | Gate | Stated | "Hi, I'm a full-stack developer with 8 years…" |
| Shopify integration shown, not claimed | Gate + 15% | Stated | "Extensive Shopify experience," nothing to click |
| Keeps working + will warn you | 30% | Stated | Nothing about after launch or platform changes |
| Plain-language what / how long / what I need, in order | 20% | Stated | Tool list; "happy to discuss on a call" |
| Problem restated in their terms | 15% | Strong | "I can build your dashboard," none of their numbers |
| Lands before the push, verified; starts now | 10% | Stated | "Done in 3 days" or "free in two weeks" |

**Not graded (or tiebreaker only):** stack ("don't care"), visual polish, portfolio breadth, years of experience, real-time data, anything beyond the five numbers.
**Differentiators:** naming the attribution over-claim and a blended ROAS as the truth number; a check the client runs themselves (two Mondays beside their Sheet); landing a week early.

## Explicit requirements
- [MUST] Five numbers in one place: revenue / orders / AOV by day, week, month; ad spend and ROAS per channel (Meta, Google); Klaviyo email revenue; top 10 products; refund rate
- [MUST] Self-updating, zero manual steps — "I don't want to click anything on Monday morning except 'open'"
- [MUST] Proposal opens with a fitness product for someone who hates the gym — literally the first words
- [MUST] Proposal answers, in this order: what you'd build, roughly how long, what you'd need from me
- [MUST] No framework list; plain language for a non-technical reader
- [MUST] Evidence of at least one prior Shopify *integration* (API/data work, not theme work) — "(show me)"
- [MUST] Done before the Black Friday push, "about 5 weeks out" from an Oct 5 post — "I'd love to" is a MUST in polite clothing
- [MUST] Price in the neighborhood of $1,500 fixed; "Intermediate" level
- [SHOULD] "Simple" — scope sized to the five numbers, nothing speculative
- [MAY] Klaviyo API experience; any stack ("I don't care what it's built with")
- Mechanics: posted 3 days ago, picks by end of this week, 20–50 proposals already — submit today

## Traps
- **Attention filter placed late**, after the requirements, to catch skimmers. It is also a mini market test: "someone who hates the gym" is *their* customer (home fitness), so a gym product (a barbell) reads as not getting it; a home product (walking pad, adjustable dumbbells, resistance bands) does.
- **"(show me)" is a proof gate.** A sentence of experience without a link, screenshot, or named store is a claim, and claims don't pass.
- **Stack trap.** "Don't care what it's built with" plus "don't send me frameworks" means every tool name spends words on an announced non-criterion. Proposing Looker Studio without addressing the breakage story is the tone-deaf version.
- **Fixed fee vs open-ended "tell me if it's going to break."** The last freelancer did fixed work, then had no reason to keep answering. If the after-care isn't bounded in writing, you are signing up for the same ending.
- **Attribution ground is unstable.** Meta, Google and Klaviyo each report revenue by their own attribution window; per-channel numbers will add to more than Shopify revenue. The client will notice on Monday one. Warn now, or lose trust then.
- **Date math.** "Before our Black Friday push" is ~Nov 9–12, not Nov 27; five weeks from an Oct 5 post, and ~4 weeks from a hire this week. Black Friday week is also the highest-volume, highest-stakes week — the dashboard must be *proven* before it, not finished on it.
- **Timing.** Posted 3 days ago, decision by end of week: a proposal sent tomorrow may be too late, and the first client reply tests reachability from message one. Notifications on, answer within hours.
- **Over-delivery.** Real-time refresh, twenty metrics, forecasting, "I'll also fix your attribution model," or a discovery phase before quoting — each blows "simple," the budget, or the "answer me now" ask.
- **Graded but looks optional:** the wit of the opener, the specificity of the access list, naming running costs, the one "what breaks" sentence, who owns the accounts.
- **Tone.** "Hi there… Thanks!" invites plain, direct prose with a little personality. "Dear Hiring Manager, I am writing to express my interest" is a mismatch they notice before content.
- **Defects in the stated list** (noticing is a differentiator): "ROAS per channel" with no truth source; Klaviyo revenue double-counts ad-attributed revenue; refund rate undefined (by count or value, by order date or refund date); top 10 by what; refresh cadence unstated; "what's actually making money" hints at margin but no COGS is mentioned.
- **Blocks review entirely:** no product opener; no Shopify evidence; price far outside $1,500; arriving after the pick.

## Decisions to make / questions to ask
There is no Q&A window, so assume and state; reserve at most two questions for the "what I'd need from you" section, where asking is sanctioned.

- **Stack** → Assume; describe by outcome, name at most one tool in a parenthesis — *Rationale: whichever you pick, the proposal should say three things: you own the data-pull code (no third-party connector — that is what broke last time), it lands somewhere they already open, and it pins to a current API version with a quarterly check.*
- **ROAS truth source** → Assume: show each platform's own ROAS *and* a blended ROAS (Shopify revenue ÷ total ad spend) as the headline — *Rationale: "what's actually making money" is a blended question; say up front the channel numbers will over-claim and why.*
- **After-care bound** → Decide: 30 days of fixes after go-live (covers BF and Cyber Monday) included; then an optional small monthly check (API-version watch, "you'll hear from me first") at a named price — *Rationale: answers the ghosting fear with a schedule, not a promise, and resolves fixed-fee vs open-ended.*
- **Cadence during build** → Decide: a short Friday update message, no meetings unless they want one — *Rationale: reachability demonstrated weekly before launch is the only evidence they can see before February.*
- **Timeline shape** → Decide: Shopify numbers live in week 2; ads and Klaviyo week 3; parallel run against their Sheet on two Mondays; go-live target ~Nov 2 — *Rationale: lands a week before the push and gives them a check they can run themselves.*
- **Refresh cadence** → Assume nightly — *Rationale: Monday-morning use; avoids rate limits during BF volume.*
- **Refund rate** → Assume refunded orders ÷ orders in period, by order date, with refund value shown too — *Rationale: cheap to state; shows you saw the ambiguity.*
- **Top 10 products** → Assume by revenue in the selected period, units alongside — *one line, stated.*
- **Default view** → Assume opens to this week vs last week, month to date; day/week/month toggle — *Rationale: "open" is the only click they want to make.*
- **Historical backfill** → Decide: 13 months — *Rationale: lets them compare this Black Friday to last year's, which is the Monday after the push.*
- **Running costs and ownership** → Decide: all accounts and credentials in the client's name; name the monthly cost even if ~$0 — *Rationale: durability that survives you is the strongest answer to the Looker story.*
- **Scope boundary** → Decide: the five numbers from the four sources; excluded: margin/COGS, other sales channels, inventory, forecasting — note margin as a cheap later add if cost-per-item is filled in Shopify — *Rationale: "simple" and $1,500 are the same test.*
- **Klaviyo** → If you have it, name where; if not, say so and that it is the simplest of the four sources — *Rationale: an honest "no" on a nice-to-have is a trust signal; a faked "yes" is the opposite.*
- **Price** → $1,500 fixed, matched — *Rationale: far outside in either direction is a veto; slightly above with a reason is fine.*
- **Ask (1):** a copy of the current Monday Sheet — *it is both the spec and the parallel-run baseline.*
- **Ask (2):** the date the Black Friday push actually starts — *fixes the real deadline.*

## Pre-submission check
- [ ] Word one is a home-fitness product; the greeting comes after
- [ ] A Shopify integration you can point at, with how long it has run, in the first four lines
- [ ] Their problem restated with their numbers: 4 hours, Monday, four sources, "what's actually making money"
- [ ] Three labeled parts in their order: what I'd build / how long / what I'd need from you
- [ ] Zero framework names, or one in a parenthesis
- [ ] One plain sentence on what breaks and what you do before it does
- [ ] Friday cadence + 30-day after-care + named ongoing option, in writing
- [ ] Attribution warning and blended ROAS named

- [ ] $1,500 with included / excluded / running costs; accounts in their name
- [ ] Access list specific enough to prove you have done this before
- [ ] Under ~300 words; reads in a minute; register matches "Hi there"
- [ ] Sent today; notifications on; reply to their first message within hours

**Sequencing:** before writing — pick the product, find the Shopify sample, draft the access list, do the date math · drafting — restatement, then the three parts, then the durability sentence, then cadence and bound, then price and scope · before sending — cut to ~300 words, check word one, strip tool names · after sending — be reachable; the test is already running.
